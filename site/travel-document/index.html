Can refugees leave the USA? Travel and the refugee travel document (I-131)

Short answer. Do not leave on your South African passport, and do not go back to South Africa, while you are a refugee. USCIS says you must apply for a refugee travel document, and attend biometrics if they are required, before you leave. Travel that looks as if you no longer need protection can put refugee status at risk. A trip in the first year also pauses the one-year physical-presence clock for the green card.

Speak to an immigration attorney or an accredited representative before any travel, including a trip that seems short. This page is general information, not advice about your case. The disclaimer says the same.

Do not use a South African passport or return to South Africa

  • The USCIS guide says refugees and asylees were given protection because they are fleeing the country of nationality, so they cannot acquire a passport from that country. The refugee travel document, also called Form I-571, is the substitute. It looks similar to a U.S. passport [source].
  • The same guide says: if your travel abroad suggests that you no longer need the protection of the United States, your status as a refugee or asylee may be terminated. Returning to the country of past persecution or of feared future persecution may have to be explained when you come back, and it can be evidence that the fear is not genuine or that protection is no longer needed [source].
  • A short stay for compelling reasons is described only as a limited circumstance, not as permission to go home for a holiday [source].
  • The guide's later answer about losing status after a green card is written about permanent residence based on asylum. It does not say the same sentence about a green card based on refugee status. That point is not stated for refugee green cards [source].
  • The instructions include a warning that cites section 208(c)(2)(D) of the Immigration and Nationality Act for asylees who applied on or after 1 April 1997. That warning is written for asylees. This page does not treat it as the refugee rule [source].

USCIS still publishes an Adjudicator's Field Manual chapter on refugee travel documents. The current Policy Manual part is linked beside it. Read both, and ask a lawyer if they do not say the same thing [source, source].

  • Three things have to be shown before USCIS treats you as having gone back to the old country's protection: you acted voluntarily, you intended to obtain that protection, and you actually obtained it [source].
  • Unless there is proof to the contrary, a refugee who applies for and obtains a national passport, or a renewal, is presumed to have intended to use that country's protection. Getting a birth record or a marriage record is not treated as that [source].
  • Having a valid travel document from the country of nationality can make Form I-131 deniable [source].
  • There is no absolute prohibition on issuing a refugee travel document for travel to the country of nationality or last residence. The same chapter says that travel may stop you establishing eligibility for permanent residence under section 209, and that each case is judged on its facts. Visiting a sick parent is weighed differently from regular holiday or business trips [source].

Passport logistics, such as how an SA passport is renewed abroad, are a different question. They are on SA passport and citizenship risks.

Apply before you leave, and attend biometrics

  • If you are in the United States in valid refugee status, you must apply for the refugee travel document before you leave [source].
  • If biometrics are required, you must appear for them before you leave. If you do not appear, USCIS may deny the application [source].
  • Applicants aged 14 to 79 who apply for a refugee travel document may have to give biometrics at an Application Support Center. If you are already abroad, that may be at a U.S. embassy, consulate or USCIS office. USCIS tells you where [source].
  • Leaving before USCIS decides usually does not affect the decision. Leaving before biometrics are collected may cause a denial [source].
  • You can ask, when you file, for the document to be sent to a U.S. embassy, consulate or USCIS office abroad for pickup. Not every post can do that [source].
  • If you are already outside, USCIS may in its discretion allow Form I-131. You must file within 1 year of your last departure and include an explanation of why you did not apply before you left. USCIS decides whether to accept it [source].
  • Part 6 of the form asks for the country your refugee status is based on, whether you plan to travel there, whether you have returned, and whether you have received a benefit, a travel document, a passport or a re-entry permit since admission. It also asks about a new nationality or refugee status in another country [source].
  • File Form I-131. The USCIS form page says a refugee travel document (Part 1, box 2 or 3) cannot be filed online. File by mail [source].
  • USCIS changed the I-131 filing location on 7 October 2026, with a grace period for forms postmarked by 9 November 2026. The instructions say to use the filing-addresses page, because a form sent to the wrong place can be rejected or delayed [source, source, source].
  • Do not put Form I-131 in the same envelope as Form I-485 [source].
  • The fee is on Form G-1055. The amount for a refugee travel document was not stated on the pages checked for this guide [source, source].

How long USCIS takes

A processing time for this document is not stated on the USCIS pages this guide quotes. Check the USCIS processing times tool for Form I-131 at the office that has your case [source].

Do not copy a single number from a historic table that groups every kind of travel document together. That is not a refugee travel document time, and this page does not quote one.

How long the document is valid

  • The Form I-131 instructions, dated 13 May 2026 in the file, say a refugee travel document is valid for 1 year and that the validity date cannot be extended [source].
  • The USCIS guide says it is valid for 1 year, or until refugee or asylee status expires, whichever comes first [source].
  • Both are official. They do not use the same sentence. Show both to a lawyer before you plan a trip near the end of the year.
  • USCIS will not issue another one while the current one is still valid, unless the old one is returned or you show it was lost or destroyed [source].
  • USCIS will not issue one for travel to an area if a Federal Register notice bars that document for that area [source].

Before the green card, and while Form I-485 is pending

  • If you have refugee or asylee status and you are not a lawful permanent resident, you must have a refugee travel document to re-enter, unless you have an advance parole document [source].
  • The USCIS travel documents page says the same for a derivative refugee, and says that leaving without the document may mean you cannot re-enter or that you are placed in removal proceedings [source].
  • If you have filed Form I-485 and you leave without an advance parole document, USCIS generally treats the I-485 as abandoned. The exceptions in the instructions are only H-1 and H-4, L-1 and L-2, K-3 and K-4, and V visas. Refugee status is not in that list [source].
  • The instructions do not say that a refugee travel document stops an I-485 being treated as abandoned. Do not assume it does. Ask a lawyer before you travel with a pending I-485 [source].

The green card steps themselves are on Green card after 1 year.

The one-year green card clock and naturalisation

Green card. Refugees must have 1 year of physical presence in the United States when the adjustment application is decided, not merely one year after the admission date on paper. Only days inside the United States count. If you travel during that first year, you meet the rule only when the time inside adds up to at least 1 year [source].

  • A principal refugee, and a derivative refugee admitted from abroad, starts counting on the date of admission as a refugee [source].
  • A derivative who was already in the United States and who gained status through an approved Form I-730 starts on the date that petition was approved [source].

The public green card page also says you must be physically present for at least 1 year after admission when you file [source]. The Policy Manual stresses the date of the decision and the days you were actually here [source]. Read both before you travel in year one.

Naturalisation is a later clock. The Policy Manual counts continuous residence from admission as a lawful permanent resident, not from the refugee admission. A trip while you are still only a refugee is not part of that later count [source].

  • Continuous residence and physical presence are separate requirements. The months of physical presence required for naturalisation are not stated on the continuous-residence chapter checked here [source].
  • An absence of 6 months or less does not, by itself, break continuous residence. Several shorter trips can still be reviewed [source].
  • More than 6 months but less than 1 year is presumed to break it. You can try to rebut that, for example if U.S. employment was not ended and you did not take a job abroad, if immediate family stayed, and if you still owned or leased a home [source].
  • One year or more breaks continuous residence unless Form N-470 is approved for qualifying employment abroad [source].
  • An approved N-470 does not remove the physical-presence requirement, except for certain U.S. government work or religious duties described in that chapter [source].

Once you have a green card, the Form I-131 instructions give a separate warning: if a permanent resident who obtained that status because of refugee or asylee status does not get a re-entry permit, lengthy or frequent absences can support a finding that permanent residence was abandoned [source]. A re-entry permit and Form N-470 are different forms. The pages checked do not say one replaces the other.

Coming back through a port of entry

  • The refugee travel document lets you seek admission. It does not guarantee it. A DHS officer at the port of entry decides admissibility when you present it [source].
  • The USCIS guide says people without a foreign passport are sent by U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) to secondary inspection, where a paper I-94 is issued [source]. The guide is describing arrival without a foreign passport. It does not say that presenting a refugee travel document skips inspection.
  • A separate page on cbp.gov for this return was not found in the sources checked. This guide does not invent a CBP link. The sentences above are USCIS sentences that name CBP and the port-of-entry officer [source, source].
  • The document is invalid if it was obtained by a material false representation or concealment, if you are ordered removed, or if the 1951 Refugee Convention ceases to apply to you under Articles 1C, D, E or F [source].

Typical traps

  • Travelling on a South African passport, or renewing one, and treating that as harmless [source, source].
  • Going back to South Africa for a holiday, a funeral or business without legal advice [source, source].
  • Leaving before the biometrics appointment [source].
  • Leaving with a pending I-485 because you hold, or expect, a refugee travel document [source].
  • Taking a trip in the first year and still filing the green card on the one-year anniversary of arrival [source].
  • Assuming the document can be extended. The instructions say the validity date cannot be extended [source].
  • Filing online, or putting the I-131 in the I-485 envelope, or using an old filing address after 7 October 2026 [source, source].
  • Applying from abroad and expecting approval. That path is discretionary, and only within 1 year of the last departure [source].
  • Asking a U.S. post abroad to hold the document when that post does not offer the service [source].

Questions and answers

Can I leave the USA while I am a refugee?

USCIS does not say every trip is forbidden. It does say you need a refugee travel document before you leave, and that travel back to the country you fled can put your status at risk. Do not leave on a South African passport. Speak to an immigration attorney or an accredited representative before you buy a ticket. Read the Form I-131 instructions and the USCIS travel documents page.

Can I use my South African passport?

No. The USCIS guide says refugees cannot get a passport from the country of nationality, and that the refugee travel document is the substitute. The Adjudicator's Field Manual says that, unless there is proof to the contrary, a refugee who obtains a national passport or renews one is presumed to have intended to use that country's protection again. See the USCIS refugee and asylee guide and the field manual chapter.

Can I go back to South Africa?

Do not treat a trip to South Africa as safe. The USCIS guide says that if travel suggests you no longer need protection, refugee or asylee status may be terminated, and that going back to the country of past persecution or feared persecution can be used as evidence. A short stay for a compelling reason is described only as a limited possibility, not as a general permission. There is no absolute ban on issuing a travel document for that trip, but the same chapter says the trip may block a green card under section 209 of the Immigration and Nationality Act. Ask a lawyer. Sources: the USCIS guide and the field manual chapter.

When do I apply?

Before you leave, and before any biometrics appointment USCIS requires. If you leave first, USCIS may, in its discretion, accept Form I-131 within 1 year of the last departure if you explain why you did not apply before you left. That is not a right. See the Form I-131 instructions.

How long is the document valid?

The Form I-131 instructions say a refugee travel document is valid for 1 year and that the validity date cannot be extended. The USCIS guide says it is valid for 1 year, or until refugee or asylee status expires, whichever comes first. Both sentences are on official pages. USCIS will not issue a new one while an old one is still valid unless you return it or show it was lost or destroyed. See the instructions and the guide.

How long does USCIS take?

A single processing time for a refugee travel document is not stated on the pages checked for this guide. Use the USCIS processing times tool and select your form and office there. Do not rely on a number quoted from a grouped travel-document table.

What if my Form I-485 is pending?

The Form I-131 instructions say USCIS generally treats a pending Form I-485 as abandoned if you leave without an advance parole document. The exceptions named there are H-1 and H-4, L-1 and L-2, K-3 and K-4, and V visas, not refugee status. The instructions do not say that a refugee travel document stops that abandonment. Ask a lawyer before you travel with a pending I-485. See the Form I-131 instructions.

Does a trip pause the one-year green card clock?

Yes. Time outside the United States does not count. The Policy Manual says the one year is physical presence at the time the green card application is decided, and that a refugee who travels in the first year meets the rule only when the days inside the United States add up to at least one year. See USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 7, Part L, Chapter 2.

What happens at the airport when I come back?

The Form I-131 instructions say a DHS officer at the port of entry decides whether you may be admitted when you present the refugee travel document. The USCIS guide says people who arrive without a foreign passport can be sent by U.S. Customs and Border Protection to secondary inspection for a paper I-94. A separate CBP page for refugee re-entry was not found in the sources checked for this guide. See the instructions and the guide.
