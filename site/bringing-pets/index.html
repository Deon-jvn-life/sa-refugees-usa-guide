Bringing your pets from South Africa to the USA

In short. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and Department of Agriculture rules for pets, how long a dog takes, and what the Embassy says about pets.

Refugees: the programme does not move or pay for pets. The Embassy page of 7 October 2026 says neither the U.S. government nor USRAP implementing partners assist in or fund pet transport. Embassy Pretoria is not working with any entity to move pets. Arrange pet travel yourself after you arrive. The Embassy recommends waiting at least 90 days, because the first housing may not allow pets, and no one in the programme will move the pet inside the United States [source].

The Embassy FAQ answers "How do I arrange for my pet to join me?" in the same way: USRAP cannot fund or assist travel for pets or emotional-support animals, and it strongly discourages bringing pets until at least 90 days after the refugees arrive. Pets limit housing. Deposits and monthly pet rent cannot be paid from resettlement support. Later you may use a shipper. Not every animal can be admitted. The process can take a long time and cost a lot [source].

Beware of anyone who says they are coordinating with the government to move a pet or to speed up your case. Report that on the USRAP Tip Form linked from the Embassy page [source]. See Avoiding scams.

How long it takes

Count back from the dog's travel date. These figures are the ones CDC states for a foreign-vaccinated dog that has been in a high-risk rabies country. South Africa is on CDC's high-risk list [source, source]. Where a page gives no time, this table says so. Do not use European or British export timetables for a trip to the United States. Those rules are not on the CDC or South African pages checked.

A realistic total, using only those figures. If the dog still needs its first valid vaccine, the earliest blood draw is 30 days later, and that draw must also be at least 28 days before entry. That is 58 days after the vaccine, and only if a CDC-approved lab returns a result CDC will accept in time. CDC does not publish one turnaround number [source, source]. The dog must also be at least 6 months old on the day it enters [source]. If there is no valid titer, add 28 days of quarantine after arrival [source].

That CDC clock is separate from the Embassy clock. The Embassy recommends at least 90 days after the person arrives before the pet travels, and the programme does not pay [source, source].

Dogs: what CDC requires from a high-risk country

  • South Africa is on the high-risk countries list [source].
  • Certification form, filled by a vet and endorsed by an official government veterinarian, completed no more than 30 days before travel [source].
  • A valid rabies serology titer from a CDC-approved lab, or a reservation that includes 28-day quarantine [source, source].
  • A reservation at a CDC-registered animal care facility, and arrival at that airport [source].
  • Microchip before the vaccine. The number is on every form [source].
  • Appear healthy. At least 6 months old [source].
  • Documents in English, or with a certified English translation [source].
  • The CDC Dog Import Form is required for each dog. After you confirm the email, the receipt is emailed. The instructions say the verification message comes from 1cdpwebsurvey@cdc.gov and the receipt arrives within 15 minutes of the first submission. Show it to the airline and to CBP [source].
  • The CDC Dog Import Form is free [source]. The direct form address is on the CDC instructions page. This guide does not copy a second address [source].

Cats

  • CDC says a cat must appear healthy and can be inspected. It can be denied if there is evidence of a disease that spreads from animals to people. If the cat is ill, a vet exam at the owner's expense may be required [source].
  • CDC does not require proof of rabies vaccination for cats. It recommends the vaccine. The destination state and the airline may require more. Hawaii and Guam have quarantine even for animals that come from the U.S. mainland [source].
  • A CDC microchip rule or a CDC waiting period for cats: not stated on the cat page checked [source].
  • USDA APHIS says it has no animal-health requirements for a pet cat. It tells you to check CDC, the Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), and the destination state [source].

USDA APHIS rules for dogs

APHIS says CDC is the main authority for dog rabies rules. APHIS adds rules if a country is affected by foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) or screwworm [source].

  • If the country is FMD-affected: fur and bedding free of excessive dirt, hay or straw; bathe the dog on arrival and keep it away from livestock for 5 days. APHIS does not require a document for that step [source].
  • If the country is screwworm-affected: a certificate, signed by a full-time salaried veterinary official of the region, that the dog was inspected within 5 days before shipment and is free of screwworm, or was infested, quarantined and treated until free. APHIS does not publish a template [source].
  • The disease-status page tells you to follow those rules if a country is "not free". On 26 October 2026 that page did not name South Africa. This guide does not say South Africa is, or is not, FMD-affected or screwworm-affected. Check the lists [source].

South Africa: the export certificate

  • The Department of Agriculture says that, apart from its Namibia guide, you contact your local state veterinarian about exports [source].
  • A U.S.-specific validity window on that page: not stated [source].
  • An older PDF still on the former department site says the provincial state vet certifies the importing country's health certificate if the requirements are met, a private vet cannot give official certification, and the exporter bears the preparation costs [source].
  • The document the government vet must endorse for CDC is the certification form above. It is a 30-day document. This guide does not invent a second South African expiry date [source, source].

Airline crate

IATA is the airline industry body, not a government. Its pet page (Container Requirement 1) says only small dogs and cats may travel in the cabin, and some airlines will not allow that and use a heated, ventilated hold [source].

  • Measure A from nose to the base of the tail, B from the ground to the elbow, C across the shoulders or the widest point, and D as standing height to the top of the head or the ear tip, whichever is higher [source].
  • Length is A plus half of B. Width is C times 2 (times 3 for two animals, times 4 for three). Height is D plus bedding. Snub-nosed breeds need a container 10 percent larger [source].
  • Weaned puppies or kittens from the same litter, not older than 6 months, up to 14 kg each, up to three in one container. Two compatible adults up to 14 kg each may share. Heavier animals travel alone [source].
  • IATA says to get the animal used to the crate a few weeks or months before the trip. It also says a light meal 2 hours before the animal is handed over is a legal requirement in the United States. That sentence is IATA's [source].

If you are going to Florida

These points are only for Florida. Other states differ. APHIS tells you to check the agriculture department of the state you are entering [source, source].

  • An official certificate of veterinary inspection is normally required within 30 days of issue. Florida lists exceptions, including a privately owned pet travelling with its owner [source].
  • Dogs and cats 3 months and older that are brought into Florida, including privately owned pets with their owners, need proof of a current rabies vaccination from a licensed veterinarian [source].
  • Inside Florida, dogs and cats 4 months and older need a current USDA-approved rabies vaccination [source].

A separate Florida notice about screwworm and travel between U.S. states is not a South Africa import rule. This page does not mix the two [source].

Refugee flights

  • The only official USRAP travel booker the October 2026 Embassy page names is RSC Africa, operated by Church World Service. The Embassy prints https://mycase.rscafrica.org/ as that partner's site. Travel booked by anyone else is not supported and can put your entry and initial help at risk [source].
  • The same page does not name the International Organization for Migration as the office that books this programme's flights [source].
  • Whether a particular RSC Africa ticket can include a pet: not stated. Ask RSC Africa, and ask your resettlement agency, before you assume the seat or the hold can take an animal [source].
  • The first 90 days service list from ORR does not mention pets [source].
  • Costs are the family's. The Embassy says pet transport is not funded. CDC says the importer pays the animal-care facility. The older South African export note says the exporter pays preparation costs [source, source, source].
